# US labelling — gap analysis, findings, and SKU scoping

Internal working document. Assesses the 5 files in the shared folder against US federal food-labelling
requirements, and proposes how to group the ~26-SKU range for quoting.

## B. Findings requiring a decision before any artwork is drawn

**F-1 — Specification document control.** Both specifications carry `Name of the Trade Mark: TEA MOMENTS`
while the product is Sherlock Secrets. Harmless internally, but a US importer or an FDA/CBP reviewer
comparing the spec to the pack will read it as an inconsistency. Correct the template before the pack
is issued.

**F-2 — The nutritional values are not usable and are probably wrong.**
Earl Grey 50 g is declared at **0.02 kcal / 100 g** and Pure Ceylon 100 g at **0 kcal / 100 g**, both
labelled "per 100 grams of product". Dry black tea leaf is in the order of 250–350 kcal/100 g; even a
brewed infusion is around 1 kcal/100 mL. These figures therefore appear to be brewed-infusion values
entered into a "per 100 g as sold" field, and they are not internally consistent between the two SKUs
either (0.02 vs 0). Under EU 1169/2011 this ambiguity is survivable; under 21 CFR 101.9 a misdeclared
Nutrition Facts panel is a labelling violation. **Resolve the basis before building any panel.**
The practical US route for plain tea is a serving of 1 tea bag (2 g) or 1 tsp making 8 fl oz (240 mL),
all values zero, using the simplified format under 101.9(f) with the "Not a significant source of…"
footnote. That is cheap and defensible — but it has to be a deliberate choice, not an inherited number.

**F-3 — The compostability claim is the single largest US exposure in this set.**
"Our teabags are compostable. Suitable for home composting" is an unqualified environmental benefit
claim. Under the FTC Green Guides an unqualified "compostable" claim requires that the item breaks down
in approximately the same time as the materials with which it is composted, **and** that composting
facilities are available to a substantial majority of consumers where the product is sold — which is
not true across the US. A "home compostable" claim additionally needs third-party certification
(TÜV Austria OK compost HOME or equivalent) and evidence covering the **whole** bag: filter paper,
thread, tag and any heat-seal fibre or staple. Three options: (a) produce the certificate and qualify
the claim; (b) narrow it to a material statement ("bag made from plant-based fibre"); (c) drop it from
the US artwork. Recommend deciding this before quoting, because it changes the scope of every tea-bag SKU.

**F-4 — There is no US responsible party.** The pack currently routes through Healthy Beverage Company
Ltd (Manchester, UK) with a Czech importer. For the US a domestic "Distributed by / Imported by" entity
and address is mandatory on every pack, and that entity is also the natural FSVP holder. Until it is
named, the artwork cannot be finalised.

**F-5 — Origin marking is undecided.** Tea is grown in India, Kenya, Sri Lanka and China; blending and
packing happen in Ukraine. CBP marking, the FDA ingredient statement, and the marketing copy
("authentic Ceylon tea") all have to tell the same story. Get a substantial-transformation
determination once, then apply it across the range.

**F-6 — Coverage in the folder is thin and does not overlap.** Only Earl Grey 50 g has both a spec and
artwork. Pure Ceylon 100 g has a spec but no artwork; both English Breakfast SKUs have artwork but no
spec. A representative review needs at least a handful of matched spec + artwork pairs.

**F-7 — Artwork was supplied as flattened JPGs.** Colour build, print finishes and coding windows are
readable, but small mandatory type, the exact back-panel layout and the available panel area cannot be
verified from a raster. Print-ready vector files (AI/PDF with live text, or a PDF/X-4) are needed to
size a Nutrition Facts panel and confirm type-height compliance.

## C. How the range should be grouped for quoting

Labelling effort scales with **composition templates**, not with SKU count. The ~26 SKUs collapse into
five templates:

| Template | Description | Example SKUs | Relative effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Single-origin unflavoured tea | Pure Ceylon, Rich Assam, Pure Green, The Finest Ceylon | Lowest — one ingredient, one origin, one nutrition basis |
| T2 | Multi-origin unflavoured blend | English Breakfast (loose, 25 bags, 100 bags) | Low — adds a multi-country origin statement |
| T3 | Flavoured black tea | Earl Grey, Bergamot Prime | Medium — flavour naming, "natural flavor" position, lemon peel as an ingredient |
| T4 | Botanical / herbal blends | Pleasure Time, Spring Feeling, Alpine Meadow, Green Melissa, Jasmine, Milk Oolong | **Highest** — multiple botanicals (rose petals, orange flower, lemongrass, thyme, melissa, mint, apple), identity question ("tea" vs "herbal infusion"), allergen and heavy-metal screening, possible structure/function claim exposure |
| T5 | Format variants of an approved template | 100-bag versions, 180 g large loose packs | Lowest — panel re-flow and net-quantity change only |

Once a template is cleared, each additional SKU inside it is a variant, not a new project. This is the
basis on which volume pricing for the remaining SKUs should be requested.

### Proposed representative first block (8 SKUs, covering all five templates)

| # | SKU | Format | Template | Why it is in the block | Already in folder? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pure Ceylon | 100 g loose | T1 | Simplest case; spec already issued | Spec only |
| 2 | English Breakfast | 100 g loose | T2 | Multi-origin blend, loose format | Artwork only |
| 3 | English Breakfast | 50 g / 25 bags | T2/T5 | Bag format + the compostability claim | Artwork only |
| 4 | Earl Grey | 50 g / 25 bags | T3 | **Only complete pair available**; flavoured reference case | Spec + artwork ✅ |
| 5 | Pure Green | 100 g loose | T1 | Green tea — separate nutrition/origin basis (China) | No |
| 6 | Pleasure Time | 22 sachets | T4 | Most complex composition in the range | No |
| 7 | Bergamot Prime | 180 g loose | T3/T5 | Large loose format, second flavoured case | No |
| 8 | Milk Oolong | 100 g loose | T4 | Oolong identity + dairy-suggestive name (real US risk: "Milk Oolong" with no milk) | No |

Note on #8: "Milk Oolong" contains no milk. In the US this raises both an allergen-adjacent consumer
expectation question and a potential misbranding question under 21 USC 343(a). It should be reviewed
early rather than late.

## D. What TeaSun must produce to unblock a quotation

**Immediately available / low effort**
1. Specifications for the remaining 24 SKUs, in the same template as 12391211 / 12381106.
2. Print-ready vector artwork (AI or PDF/X-4, live text) for the 8 SKUs above, replacing the JPGs.
3. Carton and case dimensions, units per case, cases per pallet — already in the catalogue.
4. IFS Food v8 certificate, ISO 9001 certificate, FDA food facility registration number.

**Requires work**
5. A decision on the nutrition basis (F-2), then either lab COAs or a validated calculation per RACC.
6. Signed allergen declarations per raw material + a facility cross-contact statement (F-7 / item 7).
7. Compostability certification for the tea-bag construction, or a decision to drop the claim (F-3).
8. Nomination of the US responsible party and FSVP holder (F-4).
9. Substantial-transformation / origin-marking determination (F-5).
10. Lot-code and best-before format for the US (avoid ambiguous DD/MM vs MM/DD; recommend an
    unambiguous alphanumeric date such as `BB 15 FEB 2028`).
11. Heavy-metals screen on the T4 botanical SKUs (Prop 65 exposure).
