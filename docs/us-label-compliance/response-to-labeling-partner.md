## 3. Nutrition Facts information and the underlying compositional data

**This is the largest gap, and we would rather flag it than let you discover it.**

- **No US-format Nutrition Facts panel exists for any SKU**, and none of the current artwork carries a
  nutrition panel at all.
- What exists is an EU-style energy and macronutrient declaration inside each specification, stated
  per 100 g of product:

  | | Energy | Fat | Carbohydrate | Protein |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Earl Grey 50 g | 0.02 kcal / 0.1 kJ | 0 g | 0.003 g | 0.001 g |
  | Pure Ceylon 100 g | 0 kcal | 0 g | 0 g | 0 g |

- **We do not believe these figures can be carried into a US panel as they stand.** They are labelled
  "per 100 g of product", but dry black tea leaf is in the order of 250–350 kcal/100 g, and even a
  brewed infusion is around 1 kcal/100 mL. The values therefore look like brewed-infusion figures
  entered into an "as sold" field, and they are not consistent between the two SKUs (0.02 vs 0).
  We are re-establishing the basis internally.

We would welcome your recommendation on the route. Our working assumption is that plain tea should be
declared on a serving of one tea bag (2 g), or one teaspoon of loose leaf, making 8 fl oz (240 mL)
prepared — consistent with the RACC at 21 CFR 101.12 — with all values at zero and the simplified
format under 21 CFR 101.9(f) plus the "Not a significant source of…" footnote. If you agree, please
quote on that basis and tell us whether you need laboratory certificates of analysis or will accept a
validated database calculation. If either the small-package or the low-volume exemption is realistically
open to us, we would like that assessed as part of the first block, since it materially changes the
cost across 26 SKUs.

## 5. Claims and marketing statements used on the packaging

Current on-pack claims fall into three groups.

**Sensory and descriptive** — low risk, but they should be reviewed for US wording:
"A traditional blend of three varieties of tea — bright malty taste of Indian Assam tea with natural
strength and fullness, deep rich velvety taste of Kenyan tea, rich honey aroma of Ceylon tea. This tea
perfectly tastes good with sugar and milk." (English Breakfast 100 g); "Earl Grey … carefully selected
rich black tea and fragrant bouquet complemented by sunny [lemon]" (Earl Grey 25 bags). Each pack also
carries a brewing instruction block.

**Composition and identity descriptors** appearing on the front panel:
"RICH BLACK TEA", "BLACK TEA WITH BERGAMOT AND LEMON AROMA", "premium quality". We expect the word
"aroma" to need re-working into US common-or-usual name and "natural flavor" conventions.

**Environmental claim — the one we want your view on first.**
The English Breakfast 25-bag pack states: **"Our teabags are compostable. Suitable for home composting."**
We understand that under the FTC Green Guides (16 CFR 260.7) an unqualified compostability claim carries
a real substantiation burden in the US, and that a home-composting claim would need third-party
certification covering the entire bag construction — filter paper, thread, tag and seal. Please advise
whether to (a) substantiate and qualify it, (b) reduce it to a material statement, or (c) remove it from
the US artwork. This affects every tea-bag SKU in the range, so we would like it settled inside the
first block rather than repeated 26 times.

We would also flag two brand-level claims used in our trade materials, in case they are intended to
migrate onto US packaging or the US website: "Top 5 tea brand in Ukraine" and "No. 2 packaged tea
company in Ukraine". These are substantiable but competitive, and we assume they would need review
before any US use.
