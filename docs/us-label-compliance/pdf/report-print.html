Nutrition Facts and the underlying compositional data

This is the largest gap, and we would rather flag it than let you find it. No US-format Nutrition Facts panel exists for any SKU, and none of the current artwork carries a nutrition panel at all. What exists is an EU-style declaration inside each specification, stated per 100 g of product:

SKUEnergyFatCarbohydrateProtein
Earl Grey 50 g0.02 kcal / 0.1 kJ0 g0.003 g0.001 g
Pure Ceylon 100 g0 kcal0 g0 g0 g
These figures cannot be carried into a US panel as they stand

They are labelled “per 100 g of product”, but dry black tea leaf is in the order of 250–350 kcal/100 g, and even a brewed infusion is around 1 kcal/100 mL. They look like brewed-infusion values entered into an “as sold” field, and they are not consistent between the two SKUs — 0.02 against 0. We are re-establishing the basis internally.

We would welcome your recommendation on the route. Our working assumption: plain tea declared on a serving of one tea bag (2 g), or one teaspoon of loose leaf, making 8 fl oz (240 mL) prepared — consistent with the RACC at 21 CFR 101.12 — all values zero, using the simplified format under 21 CFR 101.9(f) with the “Not a significant source of…” footnote.

If you agree, please quote on that basis and tell us whether you need laboratory certificates of analysis or will accept a validated database calculation. If either the small-package or the low-volume exemption is realistically open to us, we would like that assessed inside the first block — it materially changes the cost across 26 SKUs.

04

Allergens, origin, lot coding and durable life

Allergens

Both specifications state “No Allergen” and “Non GMO”. We recognise a bare statement is not sufficient evidence for a US position. We will provide signed allergen declarations for each raw material together with a cross-contact assessment for the Obukhiv facility, covering all nine US majors including sesame. Please confirm the format you want these in.

Country of origin

Earl Grey — Kenya. Pure Ceylon — Sri Lanka. English Breakfast — India, Kenya, Sri Lanka. Blending and packing are carried out at Trypillya Sun LLC, 46 Trypolska str., Obukhiv, Kyiv region, 08700, Ukraine. The current artwork carries no “Product of Ukraine” marking. We would like your determination on whether blending and packing in Ukraine constitutes substantial transformation for CBP marking under 19 CFR 134 — one answer applies across the whole range, and we would rather settle it once.

Lot coding

The coding field is already in the artwork, so no structural change is needed. We would like your recommendation on an unambiguous US date format — we are conscious of the DD/MM against MM/DD problem — and on whether 2.5 mm character height is adequate.

Durable life

Shelf life differs by SKU and format: 2 years for Earl Grey 50 g tea bags, 3 years for Pure Ceylon 100 g loose. Storage conditions are common across the range — dark, dry, ambient maximum 25 °C, RH below 75 %, original closed containers, odour-free storage area.

Regulatory standing

The facility holds IFS Food v8 (to November 2026) and an FDA food facility registration (stated valid to December 2026). We note 2026 is a biennial renewal year with the window running 1 October to 31 December, and we will confirm our US Agent appointment.

05

Claims and marketing statements on pack

Current on-pack claims fall into three groups. Verbatim panel copy for each SKU is reproduced on the artwork plates that follow.

Sensory and descriptive — low risk, US wording to review

“A traditional blend of three varieties of tea — bright malty taste of Indian Assam tea with natural strength and fullness, deep rich velvety taste of Kenyan tea, rich honey aroma of Ceylon tea. This tea perfectly tastes good with sugar and milk.” (English Breakfast 100 g). Earl Grey carries an equivalent bouquet description. Each pack also carries a brewing instruction block.

Identity descriptors on the front panel

“RICH BLACK TEA”, “BLACK TEA WITH BERGAMOT AND LEMON AROMA”, “premium quality”. We expect “aroma” to need re-working into US common-or-usual name and “natural flavor” conventions under 21 CFR 101.4.

The environmental claim — your view first, please

The English Breakfast 25-bag pack states: “Our teabags are compostable. Suitable for home composting.” We understand that under the FTC Green Guides (16 CFR 260.7) an unqualified compostability claim carries a real substantiation burden in the US, and that a home-composting claim would need third-party certification covering the entire bag construction — filter paper, thread, tag and seal.

Please advise whether to (a) substantiate and qualify it, (b) reduce it to a material statement, or (c) remove it from the US artwork. This affects every tea-bag SKU, so we would like it settled inside the first block rather than repeated 26 times.

We would also flag two brand-level claims used in our trade materials, in case they migrate onto US packaging or the US website: “Top 5 tea brand in Ukraine” and “No. 2 packaged tea company in Ukraine”. Both are substantiable but competitive, and we assume they need review before any US use.
